# The v2 adapter learns words on its own data and delivers coverage on everyone else's

2026-08-26. CPU only; no GPU, no paid API, no sealed holdout access.

The clean-pack contiguous adapter (`artifact-adapter-cleanpack-cont-s47`, the arm called
v2) was decoded on a sample of the corpus it trained on, against the matched base model,
on one stack. The sample was frozen on 2026-08-23 at
[`research/train-wer-v2/sample.json`](../../research/train-wer-v2/sample.json) before any
decode and was read, not re-derived.

**These levels are not comparable to the v1 training WER of 0.1313.** Different corpus,
different row length, different reference construction; the frozen sample says so itself.
The only valid comparison here is base against v2 on the same rows in the same run.

## The sample

300 packs of the 2,476 in the source manifest, 46 source meetings, 8 cities, 89 speakers,
8,115 s of audio, 19,459 reference tokens. Every row was hash-checked before decoding:
the manifest against the frozen `manifest_sha256`, and each row's audio and reference
against their frozen digests. `text_pn` and `text_p` hash identically on all 300 rows, so
the reference field cannot move the number.

Selection was frozen over the 2,476-row manifest, but training executed on **2,475**:
pack `31cba9e6d6dc28a7` carries a 480-token label and was cut by the 448-token decoder
cap. It is not among the 300, so every scored row was genuinely trained on.

## The result

| arm | WER | sub | del | ins | 95% CI (meeting-clustered) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| base whisper-large-v3 | 0.0824 | 0.0554 | 0.0141 | 0.0128 | [0.0715, 0.0953] |
| **v2 (cont_s47)** | **0.0666** | 0.0393 | 0.0161 | 0.0112 | [0.0576, 0.0764] |

Paired, meeting-clustered, 4,000 replicates:

| contrast | base − v2 | 95% CI | excludes zero |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **WER** | **+0.0158** | [+0.0098, +0.0224] | yes |
| **substitutions** | **+0.0162** | [+0.0124, +0.0205] | yes |
| deletions | −0.0021 | [−0.0080, +0.0040] | no |
| insertions | +0.0016 | [+0.0002, +0.0031] | yes, barely |

The adapter learned its training labels. The whole of that learning is substitutions:
0.0554 → 0.0393. Deletions moved the wrong way by an amount the interval cannot
distinguish from zero, and insertions moved by a sixth of a point.

## The finding: the training gain and the validation gain are disjoint

Set this next to `exp-2026-08-23-validation-canonical-stack`, which compared the same two
models on the 39 frozen validation windows. **Only the decomposition is compared below,
never the levels — that run is a different corpus on a different thread count.**

| | substitutions | deletions |
|---|---|---|
| on its **training** packs | **0.0554 → 0.0393**, CI excludes zero | 0.0141 → 0.0161, CI crosses zero |
| on **unseen** validation windows | 0.07841 → 0.07724, nothing | **0.09546 → 0.04357**, halved |

The two gains do not overlap. On data it has seen, the adapter gets words right that the
base model gets wrong. On data it has not seen, that ability disappears entirely and what
survives is coverage: it stops dropping passages.

This is a sharper statement of a conclusion the project already reached from the
validation side alone, where the honest summary was "a LoRA on whisper-large-v3 for this
domain buys coverage rather than recognition". The training measurement says the
recognition was *learned* and simply did not generalise. That distinguishes two
explanations which the validation number alone could not:

- the recipe never taught the model new words — **ruled out here**;
- the recipe taught them and they did not transfer — **consistent with everything above**.

It does not establish which mechanism blocks the transfer. Nothing here separates
memorisation of these specific labels from a genuine skill too narrow to reach new
speech, and this measurement cannot: it is agreement with the labels the model saw.

Yes, at row and meeting level.

Athens carries **44.6%** of the net difference on 22% of the tokens. Leave-one-city-out
still spans [170, 316] with no sign reversal, so the direction does not depend on any one
council, but the magnitude does. Sparta and Samothraki move the wrong way; both are small
and neither has an interval of its own here. Xylokastro is a single pack and is reported
only so the sample is fully disclosed — it means nothing.

The sample is heavily imbalanced by construction: Chania alone is 116 of 300 packs. Every
per-city figure above is descriptive.

## Slices

| slice | packs | tokens | base | v2 | ΔWER |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1–5 utterances | 86 | 5,007 | 0.0813 | 0.0575 | +0.0238 |
| 6–9 utterances | 135 | 8,801 | 0.0782 | 0.0658 | +0.0124 |
| 10+ utterances | 79 | 5,651 | 0.0899 | 0.0759 | +0.0140 |
| occupancy t1 (most pause) | 100 | 6,677 | 0.0878 | 0.0683 | +0.0195 |
| occupancy t2 | 100 | 6,522 | 0.0872 | 0.0761 | +0.0112 |
| occupancy t3 (densest) | 100 | 6,260 | 0.0716 | 0.0550 | +0.0166 |

The gain is largest on packs made of few turns and smallest on packs of many. That is the
opposite of what a "learned to survive pauses" story predicts, and the occupancy axis is
not monotone, so neither axis supports a mechanism. Both are reported as prespecified
diagnostics, not as findings.

## Stack

One machine, one decoder, both arms. CPU, int8, 4 threads, the frozen `DA.CONTROL` decode
config, `ftoks` normalisation, per-segment tokenise-then-concatenate, and a per-pack
ctranslate2 seed independent of the arm. Both models hash-checked before loading:
v2 `0c6976f120f12f7c`, base `bba445638b80555f`. 300/300 rows decoded on both arms with
zero failures and zero retries. Decode wall time 7,375 s and 7,279 s.

Two choices in this run are deliberate and worth stating:

- **The base arm is the local `ct2-base` build, not the Systran published conversion.**
  The two are different binaries. `ct2-base` and `ct2-v2` were written five minutes apart
  on 2026-08-23 with identical `config.json` and identical `model.bin` size; pairing v2
  against a conversion produced by someone else would have confounded adaptation with
  conversion lineage.
- **4 threads, not the 16 of earlier runs.** Measured first: at 16 threads the decoder
  ran at RTF 0.633 and at 4 threads at 0.591, so the reduction cost nothing and left the
  machine usable. Both arms used 4, and the levels here are not compared with any
  16-thread number.

`device` and `compute_type` were added to the two decode-state files after the run
completed; the harness had them hardcoded to `cpu`/`int8` and no other value was
reachable. The backfill is recorded inside those files.

## What this does not say

- **Agreement with training labels, not fidelity to audio.** A low number can mean the
  model reproduced flawed labels.
- **It cannot select an arm.** Validation does that. This is a fit diagnostic.
- The 44.6% Athens concentration is descriptive. No cause is established.
- The v2-versus-base contrast measures adaptation on this corpus. It says nothing about
  v2 against v1, which were never compared on these rows.
