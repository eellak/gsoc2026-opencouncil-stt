# Dense 300-step screen: stopped, with a real directional signal

## Decision

**`SCREEN — STOP`.** Dense packing plus higher useful-token density reduced mean
validation WER from 15.31% to 14.76% (`Δ=-0.55` absolute WER points) and reduced
deletions by 0.57 points. All three paired seeds improved. The arm nevertheless
fails the frozen promotion gate because insertions rose by 0.37 points and the
result is too concentrated in individual windows. No medium or full GPU stage is
authorized.

This is a stopped screen, not evidence that dense training is generally harmful.
It is evidence that the mechanism can recover words under a fixed 300-update
budget, but the present recipe does not control extra words well enough.

## Frozen result

The comparison used the frozen 39-window validation set: 31 meetings, 11,911
reference tokens, OpenCouncil-agreement references. A was isolated utterances and
B was the same source rows packed into dense examples. Both arms used the same
base, LoRA recipe, 300 optimizer steps, three seeds and one CUDA float16 decode
stack.

| Metric | A mean | B mean | B - A |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Validation WER | 0.15311 | 0.14759 | **-0.00551** |
| Deletion rate | — | — | **-0.00568** |
| Insertion rate | — | — | **+0.00369** |
| Training-sample WER (diagnostic) | 0.20946 | 0.21650 | +0.00704 |

Per-seed validation `ΔWER` was -0.00806, -0.00537 and -0.00311. The confidence
interval for each individual seed crossed zero; the three-seed screen was designed
as a directional gate, not as a confirmation.

Passed gates: mean WER direction, 3/3 seed direction, deletion direction and no
leave-one-window-out sign reversal. Failed gates: insertion delta below +0.0005
and no window contributing more than 25% of the net gain.

## Post-hoc insertion diagnosis

The deterministic window-level replay found 132 net extra insertions across the
three seeds. One approximately 150-second window contributes 100 of them (75.8%).
That window also has unusually high insertion counts across nearly every cached
external ASR system, which makes a reference/audio mismatch or a general long-form
pathology plausible. This is not proof of audio truth without listening.

Removing that window after seeing the outcome would violate the frozen protocol,
and it would not fully repair the gate: the remaining mean insertion delta is
about +0.00091. Changing the minimum-edit alignment tie-break also leaves the
delta between +0.00336 and +0.00369, so the failure is not mainly an alignment
artifact. On the frozen 300 isolated training clips B likewise emits 8–20 extra
insertions per seed, supporting a smaller global insertion shift beyond the one
validation outlier.

Two tempting repairs are already ruled out as immediate next experiments. The
packer already places 0.4 seconds of synthetic silence between utterances. A prior
timestamp-supervised packed arm was worse than the no-timestamp packed arm, so
timestamps are not an evidence-backed repair here.

## What follows

The blind audit is complete. Both insertion-heavy validation references contain
**material omissions**, so they require audio-faithful repair before apparent model
insertions can be separated into recovered speech and genuine over-continuation. The
36-item training audit produced only 7 fully confirmed label-and-boundary rows, one
definite boundary failure and 28 unresolved rows.

Do not buy another dense GPU run yet. Repair the two references, then execute the
accepted clean-core plus protected audited hard/name/boundary branch with an
equal-source-hours control. Any future dense mixture needs a new freeze and must
control labelled-speech exposure. The audit explains the concentration but cannot
retroactively promote this stopped screen.

Aggregates are in `eval/results_dense_screen_300.json`. Window ids, hypotheses and
token-level alignments remain only in the private cache because they contain council
speech.
