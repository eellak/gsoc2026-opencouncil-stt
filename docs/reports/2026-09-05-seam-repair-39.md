# Four seam arms on 39 windows: one deletion improvement, on a base that is not shipping

2026-09-05 (written up 2026-09-06). Record: `exp-2026-08-20-seam-repair`.
Preregistration:
[`docs/specs/2026-08-20-seam-repair-prereg.md`](../specs/2026-08-20-seam-repair-prereg.md),
frozen 2026-08-20 with five amendments, all dated before the decode.
Aggregates: [`eval/results_seam_repair.json`](../../eval/results_seam_repair.json)
(identical to `~/.cache/oc-public/seam-repair-2026-08/results-eval.json`, which is the
file every number below was read from).

## This is exploratory and it stays exploratory

**The 39 frozen evaluation windows generated the forced-cut hypothesis.** Arm F exists
because someone counted 16 forced cuts in `eval-P.json`, which is a decode of these same
39 windows. Re-running on them is not confirmation, and 4,000 bootstrap replicates do not
make it one — a wide interval and a narrow interval are equally exploratory when the
hypothesis came out of the data. The results file says so in its own `exploratory` field.
Confirmation would need the 247-window benchmark or fresh windows, on the same stack, in
a separate preregistration.

**Nothing is adopted.** Not F, not C, not W, not X.

## Verdict

**F is the only arm that improves arm P on deletions with an interval excluding zero,
and deletions are the axis on which P failed its own preregistered gate.** C wins more
WER and does not establish a deletion improvement. W is inside noise on everything. X is
worse than P on both WER and deletions, with both intervals excluding zero.

And all four sit on top of a base that is not going to production: **arm P was ruled out
for production by user decision on 2026-09-04** — it does not generalise to overlapping
speech. See "What P's exclusion does to these numbers" below.

## The run

39 windows, 31 meetings, 11,911 reference tokens, single decode per arm, local CPU int8
(`python 3.12.3`, `faster_whisper 1.2.1`, `ctranslate2 4.8.1`, `int8`, 16 threads).
`artifact-ct2-fixed`, sha256[:16] `8a1a3b257d0c1bdb`, recorded inside all four arm caches
and inside the cached arm P. Tokens by `ftoks`, alignment by `sdi`, meeting-clustered
paired bootstrap, 4,000 replicates, seed 7, blocks `meeting_id`. F, C and W decoded
2026-09-04; X decoded 2026-09-05 (below).

Provenance gap, recorded rather than smoothed over: the control A cache
(`decode-ablation/eval-A.json`) predates the manifest convention and carries **no model
hash and no environment block** — only the decode config, which matches. A's identity
rests on the config match and on the fact that no other model was on this machine, not on
a recorded hash.

## The contrast that means anything is against P

C, F, W and X are all modifications of arm P. An A-relative number mostly re-measures
P's known benefit over the control (P − A was −0.01142, CI [−0.02025, −0.00195]), so the
incremental contrast is the one to read. Amendment 1 item 17 of the preregistration said
this before anything was decoded.

In counts, out of 11,911 reference tokens, against P's 860 S / 664 D / 233 I:

- **F**: 851 / 645 / 231. The whole deletion effect is **19 recovered tokens**.
- **C**: 884 / 633 / 181. Fewer deletions and far fewer insertions, more substitutions.

more right than any other arm (substitution rate 0.06876, and its substitution advantage
over A excludes zero at −0.00999) and it emits the fewest tokens of any arm, 11,306. It
does not even buy WER with the omission — it lands back at the control's number.

## Deletions are the axis, and F is the only arm that moves them

Arm P is the best decoding result this project has by WER, and it **failed** its own
preregistered gate, which was on deletions
(`exp-2026-08-18-chunking-aware-decoding`). So the question these arms inherit is not
"which lowers WER" but "which stops losing speech".

Answering it as narrowly as the data allows: **F, and only F, and by 19 tokens.**

The near endpoint of F's deletion interval is −0.000089, which over 11,911 reference
tokens is **about one token**. The interval excludes zero, but it excludes it by roughly
the width of one word. C, which wins twice as much WER, has a deletion interval running
from −0.00647 to +0.00142 — it may be recovering speech and it may not be, and this run
does not decide.

Two things this table says that the headline does not:

1. **Half of F's deletion improvement is one meeting.** Drop `apr27_2026` and the
   −0.00160 becomes −0.00080. The sign never reverses across either leave-one-out, so the
   direction is not one window's artefact — but the *magnitude* is, to within a factor of
   two, and an effect already worth about 19 tokens does not survive being halved as
   anything a decision could rest on. This is the one number in the report most likely to
   be over-read, so it is stated next to it.
2. **W's deletion difference from the control is noise, demonstrated.** Its sign reverses
   under 12 of 39 window deletions and 11 of 31 meeting deletions. The file's own `stable`
   flag is `false` for that contrast, and it is the only `false` in the file.

## Arm X: the stale cache, and what the re-decode does and does not discharge

Arm X's decode cache on disk was written **2026-08-20**. Commit `46867240` (2026-09-02)
fixed the overlap geometry: `seam_repair.py` had been rebuilding the overlap layout from
`audio_seconds` **rounded to 2 dp**, which can move the final tile and change which side
of an edge a word at the audio end belongs to. Records now carry `geometry_seconds`
unrounded. X uses overlap and word ownership, so a cache from before that fix was **not
comparable** with W's 2026-09-04 decode, which was written after it.

X was therefore **re-decoded on 2026-09-05** under the current code. The old cache is
kept at `eval-X.STALE-2026-08-20.json` and the new one carries `geometry_seconds` where
the stale one has none (e.g. `140.854` unrounded against `140.85`).

The re-decode reproduced the stale decode **byte for byte: 0 of 39 windows differ in
text.** It was a real decode, not a copy — total wall time 4,714 s against the stale
run's 4,299 s, with no window matching on wall time. So the rounding never moved a tile
edge far enough to change ownership on these 39 windows. The risk was real; on this set
it did not fire.

**The ledger's standing caveat — that no arm X number may be quoted from either source —
is discharged for the 39-window numbers only.** It is *not* discharged for the pilot.
`~/.cache/oc-public/seam-repair-2026-08/pilot-X.json` is still 532,178 bytes of NUL,
every one of them; the two disagreeing pilot-X WER records (the ledger's 0.16062 /
0.14585 and `eval/results_seam_repair_pilot.json`'s 0.168 / 0.15508) still cannot be
reconciled, and no pilot arm X rate may be quoted. Whoever updates the ledger should
narrow that caveat rather than delete it.

Separately, the ledger record still reports arm X's mechanism verdict as **FAILS**. That
was the verdict on X-v1 under the criterion Amendment 5 **replaced** — not relaxed —
after measuring that a blind *core* cut is not a blind *decode* cut in an arm whose
neighbours hear across the seam (seam disagreement by boundary kind: blind 0.551, silence
0.608, valley 0.600 — blind seams disagreed least). Under the replaced criterion,
`pilot-verdicts.json` records X-v2 as **PASS**. X's problem at 39 windows is not its
mechanism; it is that its mechanism works and still costs 175 deletions.

## What the mechanisms did at 39 windows

Read from the decode caches, not from `results-eval.json`. The preregistration's
mechanism verdicts are pilot-only — `pilot_verdicts()` reads the pilot caches and there
is no equivalent verdict for the evaluation set, so these are diagnostics, not a passed
gate.

| arm | pieces | VAD silence cuts | valley cuts | blind cuts |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P / C (same segmentation) | 246 | — | — | 16 forced |
| F | 246 | 190 | 9 | 8 |
| X | 272 | 212 | 12 | 9 |

F roughly halves blind cutting: against P's 16 forced cuts over the same 207 internal
boundaries, F takes 8. The two counts are not a 1:1 mapping — once F moves a boundary
every later deadline moves with it, so F's 17 non-VAD boundaries are its own, not P's 16
relabelled. It does not eliminate blind cuts, and the preregistration already recorded
why — a model-free sweep found zero blind cuts unreachable under the strict
valley definition, because some speech genuinely has no 128 ms sub-0.35 gap in three
seconds.

X's asymmetric margin (lookback 0.5 s, lookahead 2.0 s, ceiling 27.0 s) is confirmed in
the cache: 19.5 s of lookback and 78.0 s of lookahead summed over 39 windows, exactly
0.5 and 2.0 per window. The ceiling still costs it 26 extra pieces.

Ownership, summed over 39 windows: **W** 21,591 candidates → 11,389 kept, 10,169
non-owner, 33 outside audio. **X** 12,162 candidates → 11,317 kept, 845 non-owner, 0
outside audio. Both conserve exactly.

## The one finding that outlives the base

The pilot's structural result holds at 39 windows and gets larger:

- **W − E-WT = −0.14944 WER** [−0.18254, −0.11731], deletions −0.02435
  [−0.03932, −0.01123], insertions −0.11779 [−0.15470, −0.08359], all excluding zero, no
  sign reversal, largest single meeting 20.4%.
- **E-WT − E = −0.01243 WER** [−0.02332, −0.00122].

Same decode, two merge rules. Turning word timestamps on and keeping the old
segment-level merge buys 1.2 WER points. Switching that same decode to word-level
ownership buys **14.9**. Arm E's insertion catastrophe (0.13425) was never about
timestamps; it was the merge. Insertions go 0.13357 under the old merge to 0.01578 under
word ownership.

This is the finding that transports, because it is a statement about merging overlapping
decodes, not about arm P's chunker. Amendment 1 item 16 still applies: `W − E-WT` is
word-level against segment-level merge holding the decode fixed, `E-WT − E` is the total
effect of enabling word timestamps under one merge rule, and neither is a clean
"timestamps only" contrast.

## What P's exclusion does to these numbers

Arm P is out for production as of 2026-09-04. F, C, W and X are all edits to P's
chunking policy, and the deltas above are deltas **on top of a base that will not ship**.

What that costs:

- **F's deletion win prices a repair to a defect that is not going into production.** As
  a product number it is worth nothing. As evidence it still says something: F differs
  from P only in the forced-cut branch, so it is the closest thing this project has to a
  causal handle on whether blind cuts cause deletions, and the answer is "yes, by about
  19 tokens on 39 windows, half of them in one meeting". The handle is not clean —
  Amendment 1 item 5 records that byte-identity with P holds only up to the *first*
  repaired boundary, after which every later deadline moves.
- **C says a seam prompt mostly buys insertions back**, not speech. That is a fact about
  `initial_prompt` across a chunk boundary and would carry to another chunker, but it is
  not established as a deletion improvement anywhere.
- **X says buying overlap margin out of the decoder ceiling is a bad trade.** 27.0 s of
  core plus margins costs 26 extra pieces and 175 extra deletions against 41 fewer
  substitutions. That generalises to any design considering the same trade.
- **W's ownership finding is the only result here that is about a mechanism the project
  will plausibly use again**, and it is independent of P entirely — it is a property of
  merging overlapping decodes.

The honest summary is that this run spent about six CPU hours to price four modifications
of a policy that had already been ruled out, and the residue worth keeping is three
mechanism statements and one merge rule, not a WER table.

## Limits

- **Exploratory by construction.** These windows generated the hypothesis. No arm is
  adopted, no gate is evaluated, and the four frozen 2026-08-17 gate conditions do not
  decide adoption here.
- **No leave-one-out on WER.** The domination check exists on deletions only.
- **Agreement, not fidelity.** This harness measures agreement with OpenCouncil's
  published text. An arm that recovers real speech the published text omits is charged an
  insertion (`exp-2026-08-17-insertion-fidelity`: 23.7% of the adapter's scored
  insertions sit on words a human heard). C's insertion win and W's insertion win are
  both partly unreadable for this reason.
- **One decode per arm.** No repeats, so decoder nondeterminism is not in the interval —
  the bootstrap covers window and meeting sampling only. The pilot's incidental finding
  that three arms were byte-identical across two runs is 5 windows on one machine, not a
  determinism guarantee.
- **Common random numbers are weaker than the phrase suggests.** F and C keep P's exact
  seeding (one `set_random_seed` per window, before the piece loop) to stay comparable
  with the cached P; with temperature fallback, arms with different piece counts consume
  different random streams.
- **The known text-assembly defect is unfixed and deliberately so.**
  `combine_piece_transcripts` joins segment strings with no separator; per-segment strip
  plus space-join changes tokenization in 7 of 39 windows on the cached P decode. Fixing
  it would make every number here incomparable with the published V / P / E table.
- **W's ownership is one owner per candidate, not per spoken word.** Two decodes of the
  same boundary word can land on opposite sides of a tile edge, producing a duplicate or a
  hole. Measured on the pilot at 12.97% mean seam disagreement; the 39-window
  seam-disagreement diagnostic was recorded per window in `eval-W.json` but not
  aggregated for this report.
- **X's segmentation is not P's or F's.** Its ceiling differs, so no difference between X
  and F is attributable to the margin alone.
- **The sealed sets stayed sealed.** 7 temporal-holdout windows in the freeze manifest and
  16 locked benchmark windows in `CLAUDE.md`; neither was touched.
